\chapter{The Road}

% % Replace with a short epigraph and its attribution.
\epigraph{Walking slowly is still moving forward.}{A familiar saying}

% % Replace with the opening scene: where the chapter begins.
Morning begins with a warm cup on the table and a short list of tasks that
fits on an index card. The first person up opens the window, looks at the
quiet street and decides that doing three things well is enough for today.
As the house likes to say, \enquote{small things, done with care, go twice as far}.

% % Replace with the development: what changes during the day.
By midmorning the plan has shifted: a brief visit, a long phone call and an
errand no one expected. Instead of rushing, the notebook gets one new line
and the afternoon finds its shape again. The trick is to move only what
needs moving and leave the rest for tomorrow without guilt.

% % Replace with the closing: what remains at the end of the day.
At night the list shows two completed marks and one task still open. It is
not a great victory, but it is an honest measure of the day. The notebook's
last line says that tomorrow the road continues, in the same shoes and with
a little more calm.
